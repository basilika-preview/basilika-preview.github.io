Lore

Step into the Interstitium and uncover the layered history of Somnexa, its Archetypes, and the stories that manifest in every match.

Welcome to the Interstitium

You are a Historian, tasked with the discovery and documentation of Somnexa's layered history. Before you is a Game that reveals the very fabric of the world.

The true nature of Basilika is not known: it not only reveals the stories of ages, but shapes them. Historians have uncovered histories depicted by games, as well as storybook rhymes, myths, local legends, and even stories of fiction. The ruleset itself is presented as “the rules as we have been given, pieced together and passed down by generations of tireless work.” The Historians' duty was the discernment of the Game; the task is exact.

The Historian's craft

Basilika is framed as more than a game — it is an act of discovery. The world's stories are not invented at the table; they are uncovered. Those who play are cast as Historians, illuminating histories through the act of play itself. “It was never random.”

Each match “manifests” a story and a history. Basilika is described as being as versatile as the storytelling and history it manifests, and so it can be played on any board shape players want to experiment with. Among established Historians, such experimentation is encouraged — it “has a habit of illuminating the most obscure stories our world has to offer.”

Archetypes: mantles and echoes

Archetypes are the core of a player's strategy and the first tile placed. In the fiction, they are mantles — identities and legacies that are created, granted, shed, and taken up again across eternity. Choosing an Archetype is choosing whose half-remembered story you will bring back into the light.

Dice of Will: the pieces

The game's pieces are Dice of Will. What (and who) each die represents is not fixed — it depends on the specific game being played and the history being uncovered. Each type of die corresponds to a Piece Type:

The Seventh Piece

A seventh piece lingers at the edge of understanding. It shares the D10's form but bears a multiple of ten. Its identity and capabilities are deliberately unknown — an open mystery seeded for future discovery.

The Classic Set and echoes of others

Each Piece Type has a distinct Class associated with it. As of the foundational games, only the Classic Set has been discerned. “Echoes of others have been heard, but only through engaging Basilika may we discover them completely.” The existence of further, yet-undiscovered Classes is part of the game's ongoing narrative frame.

The Numenolith

Among the world's symbols is the Numenolith — a singular, world-shaping tile. Only one may exist on the board at a time: if a Numenolith tile is on the Board, no other Numenolith tiles may be played. Its name and uniqueness mark it as a landmark of particular significance within a game's unfolding history.

Circuits

The world is woven together by Circuits — paths that connect Nodes of like color across the landscape. Dormant until linked, a Circuit becomes active when two Nodes of the same color align, and its influence then flows across every tile in the connected path. A Circuit is fragile: rotate a tile on its path or place a wall across it, and the connection goes dark.

A living board

Once a tile is played, it ceases to belong to any one player — it becomes the Board's tile, shared landscape open to whoever stands upon it. The world of Basilika is collectively built and collectively contested, each placed tile adding to a history no single Historian fully owns.
