How to Play

In Basilika you build two things: your deck, and the battlefield itself, laid tile by tile. Then you outmaneuver your opponent across it to capture their King. Here's how a game flows.

The core play loop

  1. Build your deck and choose Classes

    Pick one Archetype tile to anchor your strategy, then build a deck of unique cards (no duplicates): 50 to 60 for a standard 7x7 game, 30 to 40 for Blitz 5x5, or 10 to 20 for Super Blitz 3x3. Choose a Class for each Piece Type before the game begins.

  2. Set up the board

    Everyone rolls a D20; the highest roll chooses to go first or last, and play runs clockwise. On a 7x7 board, the last player rolls a D6 for the center space. Place Archetypes in opposite corners, then, in turn order, set your King and Retinue on yours. Draw 6 cards and Mulligan up to 4.

  3. Start your turn

    Gain 3 Actions (3A), then draw: 2 cards in the Early Game, 1 in the Mid-Game, and none once End-Game begins. In a one-on-one game, the first player draws only 1 card on their first turn.

  4. Grow the board

    Play Tiles into open spaces adjacent to any tile stemming from your Archetype, so your landscape spreads outward. Pay costs in Actions, Discards, or Piece HP. Modifiers attach to Classes to change how a Piece Type behaves.

  5. Move and attack

    For 1A, a piece may either Move or Attack once per turn: a Piece Action. Each Piece Type moves its own way, and the black and white squares of each tile shape where it can go. See "How your pieces move" below.

  6. Use tile effects

    Every tile is a tool. Move a piece onto a tile to trigger its effects, and bring your King onto tiles with King-Landing rewards. Once played, a tile belongs to the Board, so any player who stands on it can use it. See "Make the board work for you" below.

  7. Resolve combat

    An attack deals damage equal to the attacker's Value, which is also its HP. Reduce a piece to 0 HP to Capture it, and your attacker takes its square. Damage that falls short lowers the target's Value. Kings take half damage from non-combat sources, rounded up.

  8. End the turn, and win

    Your turn ends when you spend your final Action. You win by Capturing an opponent's King, or by landing your King on the center of their Archetype tile.

Dig deeper

Every tile is a 3x3 pattern of black and white squares, and the Classic Set pieces read that pattern differently:

  • Pawn: Steps one square in a cardinal direction (or Leaps three), and attacks one square diagonally. It may move and attack in the same turn; its attack costs 0A.
  • Knight: Jumps in an L: two squares in a cardinal direction, then one perpendicular, over anything in between.
  • Bishop: Any distance diagonally, staying on its starting color or stopping at the first square of the other color.
  • Rook: Like the Bishop, but along cardinal lines: it keeps to its starting color or stops at the first square of the other color.
  • Queen: Any distance in any direction, but it can only attack a piece on the other color from directly beside it.
  • King: One square in any direction, or Leaps three in a cardinal direction. Losing it loses you the game.

Why color matters: Bishops, Rooks, and Queens are shaped by the squares they cross, while Pawns, Knights, and Kings ignore color unless a card says otherwise. See the full movement rules.

Tiles aren't just terrain. To trigger a tile's activated effects, a piece must stand on it: pay the effect's cost (Actions, Discards, the piece's HP, or sacrificing it). Each effect can fire once per turn, and the piece that triggers it can't move or attack for the rest of that turn, so you can move or attack onto a tile first and then activate it.

King-Landing effects trigger when your King lands on that tile, which rewards bold King play. And once a tile is played, it belongs to the Board, not to you: any player who occupies it can use its activated effects.

Learn what the card symbols and keywords mean.

Basilika has four card types. Tiles are the bulk of your deck and the only way to build the board. Classes define each Piece Type's movement, your Archetype anchors your whole strategy, and Modifiers attach to Classes to change how pieces behave.

Cards are paid for with three kinds of cost: Actions (you gain 3 each turn), Discards (cards sent to your discard pile), and Piece HP (reducing a piece's Value, which does not count as damage). Managing all three is central to a strong turn.

You win by Capturing an opponent's King or landing your King on the center of their Archetype tile. If you control multiple Kings, losing just one ends your game. In multiplayer, eliminating an opponent grants you control of three of their pieces.
